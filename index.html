CLAP: Isolating Content from Style
through Contrastive Learning with Augmented Prompts

Yichao Cai, Yuhang Liu, Zhen Zhang, Javen Qinfeng Shi
Australian Institute for Machine Learning (AIML), The University of Adelaide
Code arXiv Download PDF ECCV 2024

Our method yields more robust and generalizable vision-language representations.

Abstract

Contrastive vision-language models, such as CLIP, have garnered considerable attention for various downstream tasks, mainly due to the remarkable ability of the learned features for generalization. However, the features they learn often blend content and style information, which limits their generalization under distribution shifts. To address this, we adopt a causal generative perspective and propose contrastive learning with data augmentation to disentangle content from style. We explore both image and text augmentations, enabling CLIP-like encoders to concentrate on latent content information. Our extensive experiments demonstrate significant improvements in zero-shot and few-shot classification, alongside enhanced robustness to various perturbations. These results underscore the effectiveness of our approach in refining vision-language representations.

Key Contributions

  • 📌 Formulate the disentanglement of content and style in CLIP using a causal generative model.
  • 📌 Propose augmented prompt-based contrastive learning to isolate content information.
  • 📌 Demonstrate substantial gains in zero/few-shot accuracy and robustness across benchmarks.
  • 📌 Validate via qualitative visualizations and ablations on multiple datasets.

Key Takeaways

  • ✅ Augmented prompts (especially text) are powerful for disentangling content and style.
  • ✅ CLAP delivers robust and generalizable vision-language features, especially under distribution shifts.
  • ✅ Our framework is data efficient and plug-and-play for refining existing CLIP-like representations.
